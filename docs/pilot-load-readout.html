Rate-limit facts, re-verified

Every cap above was checked against vendor docs on 2026-08-16. Two findings correct assumptions in THREAT_MODEL.md:

  • Slack's 1-req/min cliff does not apply to us. The May-2025 restriction targets commercially distributed non-Marketplace apps; Slack states internal customer-built apps are unaffected and keep Tier 3 (50/min). This resolves the pilot plan's week-2 gate in our favor.
  • Zendesk has a second per-agent cap the model missed: 700 requests per 5 minutes per user per app, alongside the 100/min client cap â€” plus an account-wide 100 ticket-updates/min sublimit (the sim tracks all three).
  • Whether proxied vendor calls count against the framework 100/min is undocumented; the sim takes the conservative reading. Trial/sandbox accounts publish no rate limit â€” read the x-rate-limit headers on the dev sandbox empirically.
  • Guru publishes no numeric limits at all (verified absence) â€” monitor 429s, don't hardcode a guess.
  • Zendesk Search has its own budget â€” search.json responses carry a dedicated Zendesk-RateLimit-search-index header (no published number) plus a 1,000-results-per-query cap. This app leans on search hard: 4 calls per ticket boot â‰ˆ 47/min fleet-wide. A zendesk.search channel was added to api-monitor.js to watch it.

Why simulate instead of load-testing the sandbox: deliberately driving traffic to probe rate limits â€” even on a d3v instance â€” falls under Zendesk MSA Â§2.2 prohibited uses ("attempt to bypass or break anyâ€¦ rate limiting mechanism"), and Zendesk offers no load-test approval channel.
